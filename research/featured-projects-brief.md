## How to read this brief

**Labels**
- **Event-level:** a public fact about the event, its host or other participants, from a real source. It's context, never a measure of Zay's work.
- **Self-reported:** Zay's own words, once he supplies them.
- **Unconfirmed:** comes from the AI-made deck. Stays out of the copy until Zay confirms or corrects it.

**Sources.** Every milestone has a source URL and a date. The date given is the source's publication date, or the date of the on-stream moment where a video upload records it. All pages were read on September 28, 2026.

**Public mention of Zay: none found for any of the four projects.** Each section notes where I looked.

**Field mapping** to `projectCopy` in `site/src/content/artist.js`: role label → `role`, standfirst → `headline`, about → `summary`, contribution → `contributions`. The current `projectCopy` text comes from the deck and should be replaced once Zay fills in the placeholders.

### Guardrails, including after Zay confirms

1. Event-wide metrics (hours watched, peaks, subscriber records) appear only as labelled event context. Never put them next to an "I" statement or phrase them as results of Zay's work.
2. Staged stream storylines are entertainment. Describe them that way; no real-crime framing.
3. No company-wide or event-wide credit unless Zay states it. The Scene/Coachella, TwitchCon and Twinathon are MemeHouse company productions, and Mafiathon 3 is Kai Cenat's production.
4. No license or endorsement has been established for third-party logos. Voxel marks are unofficial editorial treatments that identify the project. Captions don't imply affiliation.
5. Original imagery must not show the likeness of real people (Kai Cenat, other creators, talent) without their permission.
6. No superlatives, no audience numbers attached to Zay, no "viral".

### Voice

Calm, specific and plain, like an A24 press kit. Use past tense for finished work and present tense for KEON. Keep sentences short and let the facts carry it. Avoid "iconic", "record-breaking" (except inside a record's official name), "game-changing", "legendary", exclamation marks and emoji.
